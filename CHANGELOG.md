## Unreleased

The configuration key `ctl_cis_diag` (default off, on in the profile `ctl_iso`) counts the packets a Central hears in the subevents of its streams: all of them and the ones with a bad CRC, by the number of the subevent, by data channel, by the length of the payload the Central sent, and the length the header of a bad packet gives (`ll_air.CisBad`, `cis_note_heard`).  A build with the key off holds one entry of the table of channels and none of the code.  310-octet SDUs at 20 ms against the SoftDevice Controller: 20 to 28 bad of about 3,000 packets in 20 seconds, singly, over the channels, in the subevents of two long packets and none in the one of short packets.

The Host's port can take a packet whole.  `ctl_hci.HostPacketTx` (`idle`, `start`) and `ctl_hci.ctl_tx_packet` hand the packet at the front of the queue to a transport that sends it by itself, and take its slot back when the transport says it has gone; `ctl_pool.pool_tx_begin`, `pool_tx_flight`, `pool_tx_end` and `pool_tx_ready` are the pool's side, and an ACL packet the Host's buffer size cuts goes in pieces, each with its own header.  The octet interface (`ctl_tx_next`, `HostTx`, `ctl_tx_drain`) stays.  A receiver of two BISes with its extended scan active lost 4.8 to 9.0 per cent of the SDUs of each BIS (5 of 5 runs) while the stream moved three octets a turn; with the packets handed whole it lost 0.00 to 0.47 per cent (5 of 5 runs).

An event of a periodic train that carries a BIGInfo starts on time, and none is given up.  `ll_air.padv_event` gave an event up only when the radio was granted less than the ramp-up and 60 microseconds before its time; the BIGInfo (85 microseconds), the PDU and its copy to the radio (163 microseconds together on the nRF54LM20) came after the check, and an event granted with less slack than that started up to 118 microseconds late and announced a SyncInfo and a BIGInfo for a time it did not keep.  The BIGInfo, and the PDU when the event is one PDU, are built when the event before ends (`ctl_hci.ctl_padv_prepare`, `ctl_padv_info_ready`, `ctl_padv_pdu_ready`, `ll_per.padv_prebuild`), while the radio is idle, and the event copies the PDU to the radio; `ll_air.padv_work_us` asks 200 microseconds of work for an event with nothing built, 100 with the BIGInfo built, 40 with both, and 60 for an event with no BIGInfo.  With two streams broadcast, 4 runs of 8 seconds read `start_err_max_us` 0 and no event given up of 148 to 151, where the commit before gave up 5 to 21 per cent of the events and the state before that started events up to 82 microseconds late.  `Rf.x_per_err_max_us` and `Rf.x_per_work_max_us` count the start error and the work of the events of a train.  Defaults +256 B and `ctl_iso` +1,312 B over the commit before.

A scan response the Host cannot be told of is not received.  `ctl_hci.ctl_xscan_rsp` and `ctl_hci.ctl_scan_rsp_rx` judged the response, which marks the device as answered so that it is not asked again, and then found no room in the pool for the report and dropped it: the Host never had the scan response of that device in the scan, and the scanner asked no more (one run in three of the scan response row of `ll_nrf54_ext_scan_check.py`).  They look at the pool first and count `dropped_events`; the scan request goes unanswered, the backoff counts the miss and the device is asked at its next advertisement.  Defaults and `ctl_iso` images: +144 B.

The key `ctl_latency` (default off) lets the Peripheral skip connection events under the latency the Central granted (`ll_link.lat_events`, [Vol 6] Part B, Sections 4.5.1, 4.5.2, 4.5.5 and 5.5).  It listens at every event until a packet with the NESN set to one has come and after an event with no valid packet; it does not skip while it has data or a control PDU to send, a PDU the Central has not acknowledged, or a procedure that waits for the peer (the encryption start, a PHY update, a termination); it listens at the instant of a Connection Update, a channel map update or a PHY update and at the event before it; and at least once in each half of the supervision timeout.  The skipped events are committed at the next event that is heard (`lat_commit`: the channels of algorithm #1, the counter and the schedule), the window and its widening are those of the event after them, and a packet queued for the peer before the commit ends the skip.  A subrated connection and a build with isochronous channels or Channel Sounding do not skip.  A Controller that sleeps (`ctl_idle_sleep`) sleeps over the skipped events, because the arbiter books the event that is listened to.  The Link has four fields more (`lat_skip`, `lat_armed`, `lat_got`, `lat_total`); the default image is 32 B larger.

An SDU over 251 octets is cut into the fewest PDUs of equal size.  `ll_iso.plan_pdu` gave 251 octets to the first PDU of a 310-octet SDU and 59 to the second; it gives 155 to both, which shortens the longest packet on the air by 38 per cent and the work of the program between two subevents with it.  A Central whose streams carry 310-octet SDUs at 20 ms lost 2.4 to 3.7 per cent of those it sent to the SoftDevice Controller before and loses 0.4 to 1.2 per cent now, and lost 3.2 to 4.4 per cent of those it received and loses 2.2 to 3.6 per cent now (four runs each, the two images alternated).  The radio of the nRF54LM20 reports 3 microseconds of work an octet instead of 1 (`OCTET_WORK_NS` of `bsp-nordic`): the work between two subevents of an encrypted stream is 1.9 microseconds an octet on average and 2.9 at most, and a gap planned for 1.0 left 32 per cent of the subevents of an encrypted Central stream starting late; with the gap the Sub_Interval of a stream is 2 microseconds an octet of its two payloads longer.  The encrypted rows lose 14 to 16 per cent before and 0.5 to 2.4 per cent now (three runs each).

The start of a packet the Controller sent on LE Coded is taken from the ADDRESS event less `Radio.tx_address_us`, not less the 376 microseconds of the receiver's event: the first anchor after an AUX_CONNECT_REQ on LE Coded and the AUX_CHAIN_IND PDUs after an AUX_ADV_IND were 55 microseconds early on the nRF54.  The transmitter ramp-ups of LE Coded on the nRF54 are 116 microseconds for both codings (the anchors of a Central that changed PHY moved by 4 and 10 microseconds), and the interframe register is 4 microseconds longer after a received S=2 packet whatever the Controller's own coding (an AUX_SCAN_REQ or AUX_CONNECT_REQ in S=8 after an S=2 AUX_ADV_IND left 146 microseconds after it).  Flash of the default profile +144 B, controller_iso +144 B.

The HCI stream of the main loop has the time to the end of a passive scan window that is under way.  `ll_arb.arb_room` answered the few microseconds of a due booking for as long as a scan window was booked, and a scanner whose window is as long as its interval is booked all the time: the loop moved three octets of the Host's packets in a turn, a synchronized receiver of two BISes handed the Host 87 to 95 per cent of the SDUs of BIS 2 and dropped the rest in the queue of two (`n_sdu_drop`).  `ctl_hci.ctl_stream_room` (`ll_arb.arb_room_stream`) leaves the stream the time to the end of the window and to the start of any other booking; an active scan and the Initiating state, which answer inside T_IFS, keep the few microseconds.  The streams of a BIG take turns at the packet pool (`ctl_hci.flush_big`, `bis_rr`), as the connected streams do.

A framed stream keeps an SDU with octets in the Host's queue when reports of lost SDUs hold it.  `ll_iso.out_room` gives the last slot of a full queue up when it holds a report of a lost SDU (`fr_start` makes up one for each SDU of a stretch of flushed payloads, up to four, and the queue holds two); the SDU that began after the stretch was dropped and counted in `n_sdu_drop`.  The SDU the Host may be reading, the first slot, is never taken.  A unit test shows the loss; the bench figures of bugs/orbit/the-novo-controller-loses-framed-sdus-when-the-sdu-interval-is-no-multiple-of-the-iso-interval.md do not change with it.

The first stream of a group is indicated so that the other streams of its group have been indicated before it begins.  `ctl_hci.cis_instant` names for the LL_CIS_IND of a stream the connection event of its LL_CIS_REQ, moved by whole connection intervals that are whole numbers of ISO intervals: eight events after the indication is queued for each stream of the group still to be created, and not before a stream of the group that was indicated already.  A Peripheral that is told of a second stream after the first has begun has to join a group that is running, and the PTS dongle did not hear the second stream in about one run in two.  The Central no longer starts the subevents of the streams after the first 16 microseconds early (`ctl_hci.CIS_LATER_TRIM_US`, `ctl_cis_start`): every stream starts on the group's grid.

An initiator counts a CONNECT_IND as sent only when the transmitter ran to its last bit.  `ll_air.init_packet` took the access address of the answer as the sign that the CONNECT_IND was on air, waited for the radio to rest and opened the Central link; a CONNECT_IND whose transmitter stopped part way (an air trace of the nRF54LM20 DK as Central shows one, 24 of 34 payload octets, no CRC) left a link that no advertiser answered, ended the Initiating state and reached the Host a second later as LE Connection Complete with status 0x3E.  It now clears the events of the advertisement after the access address of the answer, waits for the end of the packet (`Radio.wait_phyend`, bounded by the longest packet the radio was asked for) and, when none comes, counts `Rf.init_cut`, leaves the Initiating state running and answers the next advertisement with a whole CONNECT_IND.

A judgement that ends later than `ll_air.ANSWER_READY_US` (140 microseconds) after the end of the advertisement does not patch the CONNECT_IND: the answer is taken back while the transmitter ramps up (`spec_aborts`) and the next advertisement is answered.  Before, the patch ran whenever the judgement ended, and a transmitter that had started read the answer behind the program.  The wait for the access address of the answer no longer ends in `halt`: when it does not come, the answer runs to its end before the radio is stopped, so the radio is not disabled over a transmitter that is on air.  `Rf` is 8 octets larger (`init_cut`).  `Nrf54Air.wait_phyend` of `orbit/bsp` ends after the longest packet the radio was asked for and a ramp-up, where it spun 4,000,000 reads.

A connection event's time, asked between two events, is counted from the event due next.  `ll_link.link_event_time` took the anchor of the event under way whenever an anchor had been heard or sent, and a link keeps that anchor after the event ends, so the program's poll between two events counted from the anchor of the event that had ended: the answer was one connection interval early.  `ctl_hci.cis_place` and the LL_CIS_IND of a stream whose indication waits for room in the control queue ask from the poll, so that indication gave the stream a first anchor one connection interval before the instant it announces (a test with a connection interval of 50 ms and two Source-only streams of 10 ms: the second stream's first subevent at 808,264 microseconds against 858,264).  With an ISO interval that divides the connection interval a Peripheral finds the same grid either way; with another it listens at the wrong time.  `link_event_time` reads the anchor only while the event is open (`Link.ev_open`) and counts from `Link.due` otherwise; a call inside an event, which `iso_ind` makes for a Peripheral and `iso_rsp` for a Central, answers as before.  The image `ctl_iso` of `orbit/ble` is the same size (431,940 B of text and data, 103,044 B of RAM); the function is 2 bytes smaller.  The air trace of the second CIS that fails with the PTS dongle (`bugs/orbit/the-novo-controller-fails-to-establish-the-second-cis-of-a-two-cis-group-with-the-pts-dongle-status-0x3e.md`) shows this defect is not the cause: the second stream's first subevent is on its instant there, and the filing stays open.

## 0.1.3 — 2026-11-16

2026-11-16: the helper of `tests/ctl_air_tests.nv` that synchronizes a receiver to a group sets no output data path on the BISes of a group that is lost in the turn that established it, as the Controller has told the Host of both events since 0.1.3; the suite passed only under the default configuration, which links no groups, and failed under every configuration that does.  `novo test` now passes under the minimum and the maximum configuration and under each file of `configs/`.  No source changed.

A connection event of the Peripheral carries more than one full data PDU.  `ctl_hci.forward_acl` copies the octets of a received PDU into the Host's buffer in one pass, `ctl_pool.pool_put_run`, where it called `pool_put` at each octet: 547 microseconds for the 251 octets of a full PDU on the nRF54LM20 (2.2 microseconds an octet); the whole work after a full PDU took 565 microseconds on average before and 141 after (243 at most, against a limit of 244).  The Link Layer ends an event when the work after a packet is not done 244 microseconds after the answer (`ll_air.chain_late_us`, LE 2M), and the copy alone was over that, so every full data PDU ended its event: one full PDU in each event, the same rate at every ATT_MTU.  With the copy fast, an upload to the firmware update demo through a Central with four requests in flight went from 4.46 to 5.74 kB/s at an interval of 45 ms, and the events that were cut by late work went from 0.87 to 0.01 each.  Filed as `bugs/orbit/ll-event-ends-after-every-full-data-pdu.md`.  `pool_put_run` is new and public in `ctl_pool`; `pool_put` is unchanged.  The Controller still takes three data PDUs from the air in an event (the pool has four packets and the Link Layer refuses one when fewer than two are free): the pool of eight packets lifted the same upload to 7.1 kB/s on the bench, at 1,032 bytes of RAM more, and is not made.

A Peripheral gives the Host the data of a packet it took as new even when the answer to the packet did not start.  `ll_air.conn_exchange` took a packet as new (`link_rx_fast` moved the NESN), found that the radio had not started the answer, counted a ramp timeout and returned before it read the packet; the Central sent the packet again in the next event, where it was a packet the Controller had, acknowledged and dropped, so the Host lost an ACL Data packet that the Controller had acknowledged.  A byte stream over GATT (the SMP service of the firmware update demo) had a hole in it and every request after the hole was read from the wrong octet.  The read of the packet and the call that queues what it carries are one function now, `ll_air.take_data`, which the normal path, the case of the answer that did not start and the Central's exchange call.  Measured on the nRF54LM20 DKs with the Controller as it was before the change above, a burst of 31 ATT write commands, four requests in flight, lost 8 to 15 requests in each of 13 runs; with this change none in 3 runs, and none in the bursts of the Pixel's shape (400-octet requests).  The Controller image `hci_uart` is 80 octets smaller (230,368 B), the image with `controller_iso` 32 octets larger (426,816 B); .data + .bss are unchanged.  Filed as `bugs/orbit/a-burst-of-att-write-commands-loses-the-last-small-write-between-the-pc-and-the-demo-host.md`.

## 0.1.1 — 2026-11-10

This release needs `noble-hci` 0.1.1 (the module `hci_cs`), `noble-radio` 0.1.1 (the module `ll_cs_radio`) and a toolchain 0.24.1 or later.

2026-11-09: the package reads its configuration as `noble_controller.CONFIG` (`use noble_controller.CONFIG`, `noble_controller.CONFIG.CTL_LINKS`) in place of the module `noble_controller_config`.  The keys, their values and the constants the build writes are unchanged; only the name in the source moved.  A program that wrote `noble_controller_config` builds for one toolchain release with the warning E2947 at each `use` line; write `use noble_controller.CONFIG`.  The source needs a toolchain 0.24.1 or later, the first release with the spelling.

A Central starts the subevents of every stream after the first of its group 16 microseconds before their time on the group's grid (`ctl_hci.CIS_LATER_TRIM_US`, applied by `ctl_cis_start`).  The PTS dongle, as the Peripheral, places the first event of a later stream from the earlier stream's timeline and listens in a window of about 40 microseconds that lies before the nominal time; with the trim the second stream of a group of two failed to establish (LE CIS Established 0x3E) in 2 of 12 runs of four BAP/UCL/STR rows against 12 of 16 without it.  The trim is measured against that Peripheral only.

The Link Layer keeps a record of the control PDUs of the Data Length Update procedure (`ll_link.dle_note`, `dle_rec_field`): the newest LL_LENGTH_REQ and LL_LENGTH_RSP this end queued and the newest it heard, each with the connection event, the four values and an outcome code (taken, out of range, answered no request, queue full), the newest refusal with its opcode and reject code, the kind written last, and five counters.  The record is 10 words, 6 counters and the kind written last in each `Link` (3,144 to 3,288 octets in the build report of the rig image) and is read through the image's status line (`ll dle`).  No behaviour changes.

`ll_cs_plan.sync_ns` takes the length of a CS_SYNC packet from `ll_cs_radio.cs_sync_bits` and `cs_sync_bit_ns`.  The figures do not change (44 microseconds on LE 1M and 26 on LE 2M and LE 2M 2BT for an access address alone).  The profile `ctl_cs` turns `ctl_cs_mode3` and `ctl_cs_rtt_seq` on, so the Controller's tests of mode 3, of the sounding and random sequences and of LE 2M run under it; a radio that does not report them still cuts them out of the capabilities.

`ll_cs_plan.gen_new`, `gen_instantiate_test`, `gen_begin_procedure`, `plan_new`, `plan_subevent` and `plan_arm` are public, so that a
program that drives a radio without the Controller (the Channel Sounding bench rig of `orbit/ble`) can plan the subevent of an
`LE CS Test` and hand it to a `CsRadio`.  No behaviour changes.

`ll_cs_air` is the Channel Sounding slot of the radio program: `cs_poll`, which an image's main loop calls after `ll_poll` with the planning storage (`ll_cs_plan.CsState`) and the record (`ll_cs_air.CsAir`) the image holds, reads the radio's capabilities once, runs the subevents of an `LE CS Test` and plans and runs the subevents of the procedures of a connection at the times the Start fixed, on any radio that implements `Radio`, `Timebase` and `CsRadio`.  It is removed with `ctl_cs` 0, which links none of it.  `ctl_cs_subevent` is split into `ctl_cs_subevent_plan` (the planning, which takes milliseconds) and `ctl_cs_subevent_run`; `ctl_cs_series_open`, `ctl_cs_sched`, `ctl_cs_event_time`, `ctl_cs_test_count`, `ctl_cs_test_interval_us`, `ctl_cs_test_len_us` and `ctl_cs_result_waiting` read the state the slot schedules from.  A Central that answers a Channel Sounding Start picks the offset of the first subevent 3 ms after the anchor point when the window allows (it took the window's start), and a Peripheral answers the request with the part of its window from there on (it repeated the window).  The plan carries the peer's FAE table and the CS_SYNC antenna selection, and the storage the keys state grows by 96 octets (`ctl_cs_steps`: 136 + 96 per step).  `ll_air.stop_rx` is public.  With `ctl_cs` 0 nothing changes.

The arbiter has two classes more for Channel Sounding in a build with `ctl_cs`: `ll_arb.class_cs()` and `class_cs_next()`.  The classes of the links are `ll_arb.class_acl()` plus the slot; the constants `CLASS_ACL`, `CLASS_ACL2` and `CLASS_ACL3` are gone, since the first class of a link moves up by one in a build with the feature.  A build without it keeps every class number, and its images are the same to the octet.  `ll_cs_fit` places a Channel Sounding Start among the connection events and the streams: `schedule_ok` of the procedures no longer agrees to every Start, the Controller re-suggests the connection event, the interval of events, the subevent length and the window of offsets where Section 5.1.26 allows, and `start_from_pp` proposes a Subevent_Len of at most 5 ms.  The slot `cs_poll` books each subevent in the arbiter, gives up one that does not fit as a subevent aborted for a scheduling conflict, and no longer waits 2.5 ms after a run (`ctl_cs_room` holds the HCI stream back instead).  `ctl_cs_subevent_skip`, `ctl_cs_fits`, `ctl_cs_book`, `ctl_cs_unbook`, `ctl_cs_room`, `ctl_cs_lead_us` and `ctl_cs_event_on_grid` are new.

## 0.1.0

First release.  The portable Link Layer and HCI command handler of the noble
Bluetooth stack, extracted from `orbit/ble` (`src/controller/`): the
advertising, scanning, initiating and connection states of both roles,
extended and periodic advertising, connected and broadcast isochronous
channels, LE encryption, privacy, power control, subrating and the other LE
features of Core 6.3, and the HCI command layer over them.  The radio, the
clock, the cipher and the ECDH engine are reached through `noble-radio`; the
HCI wire is read through `noble-hci`.

The 24 `ctl_*` keys of the `orbit/ble` configuration are the schema of this
package, under the section `[noble-controller]`; the constants are read as
`noble_controller_config.CTL_*`.

The public surface is what a Host package and a chip image call: the `Controller` of `ctl_hci` with its constructor, its
HCI stream, its main-loop and judgement functions and the work it owes the chip; the radio program's entry `ll_poll` of
`ll_air` and its state; `LinkPolicy` and its default of `ll_policy`; and the records and constants those read.  The
unit tests are in `tests/` and do not ship, and every `.nv` file is what `novo fmt` prints.
